PROPERTY DISPUTES - COMPLETE RESOLUTION GUIDE

TYPES OF PROPERTY DISPUTES:
1. Title/Ownership disputes - who owns the property
2. Boundary/Encroachment disputes - wall/fence on my land
3. Landlord-Tenant disputes - rent, eviction, deposit
4. Inheritance/Partition disputes - family property division
5. Fraudulent sale/Transfer disputes

STEP-BY-STEP RESOLUTION:

STEP 1 - COLLECT DOCUMENTS (Most Important):
- Sale deed / Gift deed / Will
- Property tax receipts (last 5 years)
- Encumbrance certificate (EC) from Sub-Registrar
- Survey/Mutation records from Revenue Department
- Possession letter and society NOC (for apartments)
- Loan clearance certificate (if applicable)

STEP 2 - SEND LEGAL NOTICE:
Before going to court, send registered legal notice to opposite party.
Give 30-60 days to respond. Keep postal receipt and delivery proof.

STEP 3 - MEDIATION (Try this first):
District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) offers FREE mediation.
Faster than court - usually resolved in 2-4 sittings.
Contact: district.legal@gov.in or nearest court complex.

STEP 4 - REVENUE COURT (For agricultural land):
Revenue courts handle agricultural land disputes.
File before Tehsildar/SDM for mutation disputes.

STEP 5 - CIVIL COURT:
For title declaration: Civil Suit under Specific Relief Act
For possession: Suit for possession with injunction
For injunction (stop construction): Application under Order 39 CPC

LIMITATION PERIODS (VERY IMPORTANT):
- Possession of immovable property: 12 years
- Recovery of land from government: 30 years
- Title declaration: 12 years from date of adverse possession

RENT CONTROL (Landlord-Tenant):
- Rent Control Act varies state to state
- In Maharashtra: Maharashtra Rent Control Act 1999
- Standard Security Deposit: 2-3 months rent (not 10-12 months - illegal)
- Notice period for eviction: Minimum 1 month
- Tenant cannot be forcibly evicted without court order
- Landlord must give 30 days written notice before legal eviction

IMPORTANT TIP: Get Survey from Revenue Department first.
This is the primary proof in boundary disputes.
